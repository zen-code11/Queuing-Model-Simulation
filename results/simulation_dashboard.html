Is There a Bug in Pooled Queue? Mathematical Proof & Root Cause Analysis

The user and analysts reasonably suspected that the Pooled Queue wait time ($W_q = 0.015$ min / $0.9$ seconds) was an anomaly. Here is the definitive mathematical breakdown:

  • 1. The Arena Simulation is 100% Mathematically Accurate: In the simulation file pooled queue.doe, the server resource has Capacity = 4.0 permanently open servers. During Weekend Peak, customers arrive at λ = 0.75 cust/min with mean service time 1.58 min (μ = 0.633 cust/min). The total system workload is a = λ / μ = 1.185 cashier-workloads.
  • 2. The Offered Load is Under 30%: Divided across 4 servers, the utilization is ρ = 1.185 / 4 = 29.6%. At sub-30% load, the Erlang-C delay probability is only P(wait) = 3.6%. Over 96.4% of customers walk directly to an idle cashier with ZERO queue delay! Erlang-C theoretical wait is 0.020 min (1.2 seconds); Arena empirical simulation yields 0.015 min (0.9 seconds).
  • 3. The Asymmetry Across Frameworks: Dynamic Counter kept Counters 3 & 4 closed to save cashier hours, so only 2 counters handled the 1.185 workload (ρ ≈ 59.2%, peak counter 80.7%), producing a 56.6-second wait. Express Lane segregated small and large baskets across 3 counters, stranding cart shoppers behind Counter 1 (68.1% load) while the Express Counter was underutilized.

Executive Strategy & Implementation Playbook

Based on our multi-framework findings, the store manager should choose between two implementation paths:

  • Strategy A — The Gold-Standard Customer Experience (Framework 3 Pooled Queue):
    Adopt if store front-end architecture permits a single serpentine line with stanchions. Completely eliminates line jockeying, eliminates lane-starvation, guarantees FIFO fairness, and cuts peak waiting times by 98.3%.
    Staffing Recommendation: Schedule 3 cashiers instead of 4 during peak periods. As proven by our capacity sensitivity analysis, a 3-server pooled queue delivers a 7.1-second average wait time (cutting wait by 77.2% vs Express Lane) while saving 25% on cashier payroll ($22.5$ vs $30.0$ cashier-hours).
  • Strategy B — The Labor-Optimized Hybrid Policy (F1 Weekdays + F2 Weekends):
    Adopt if floor space cannot accommodate a serpentine queue:
    • Weekdays: Deploy Framework 1 (Dynamic Counter). Mixed basket traffic flows naturally across dynamic counters, keeping labor costs low (saving ~40% cashier hours).
    • Weekends: Switch to Framework 2 (Dedicated Express Lane). Protects small-basket transactions from cart-loading shoppers with negative binomial baskets, cutting weekend peak wait times by 44.7% vs. Dynamic Counter.

Actionable Student Rush Hour Policy

During the Weekday Student Rush (λ = 0.50, 70% small baskets), Framework 2 experienced line starvation because the express lane was overwhelmed while regular counters sat underutilized. If running Framework 2, management must dynamically adjust the express basket cutoff from N ≤ 10 down to N ≤ 6 items to restore queue equilibrium.
