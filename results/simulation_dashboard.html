The Operational Trade-off: Customer Wait Time vs. Cashier Payroll Hours

Why does Pooled Queue achieve near-zero waiting times? Because it keeps all 4 cashiers permanently open (400% capacity), operating at an average utilization of ~30%. In contrast, Dynamic Counter keeps Counters 3 and 4 closed to save cashier labor hours, operating with effectively 1 to 2 active cashiers at ~80% utilization. This creates the quintessential operations trade-off: Customer Service Level vs. Staffing Payroll Overhead.

Mathematical Verification: Erlang-C & Multi-Server Pooling Proof

The remarkable difference between the Pooled Queue ($W_q = 0.015$ min) and the Dynamic Counter ($W_q = 0.944$ min) is rigorously confirmed by Erlang-C multi-server queuing theory ($M/M/c$):

  • In Pooled Queue (c = 4 servers permanently open): With arrival intensity λ = 0.75 and mean service time 1.58 min (μ = 0.633), the multi-server load is ρ = λ / (c × μ) = 0.75 / (4 × 0.633) = 29.7%. At under 30% load, the Erlang-C delay probability is only P(wait) = 3.60%. Over 96% of customers experience zero queue. Theoretical Erlang-C Wq = 0.020 minutes (1.2 seconds). Arena empirical simulation yields Wq = 0.015 minutes (0.90 seconds)—an extraordinary alignment!
  • In Dynamic Counter (effectively c = 2 servers working): Counters 3 and 4 have NumberScheduled = 0 and are only triggered conditionally when large lines form. Running 2 servers pushes load to ρ = 59.2%. Theoretical Erlang-C Wq jumps to 0.855 minutes (51.3 seconds). Arena empirical simulation yields Wq = 0.944 minutes (56.6 seconds)!
  • In Express Lane (partitioned servers): Regular Counter 1 has 68.1% utilization because cart shoppers are segregated from the express lane. Queue buildup behind regular counters is unavoidable.

Executive Strategy & Implementation Playbook

Based on our multi-framework findings, the store manager should choose between two implementation paths:

  • Strategy A — The Gold-Standard Customer Experience (Framework 3 Pooled Queue):
    Adopt if store front-end architecture permits a single serpentine line with stanchions. Completely eliminates line jockeying, eliminates lane-starvation, guarantees FIFO fairness, and cuts peak waiting times by 98.3%. Requires scheduling 4 cashiers during rush hours.
  • Strategy B — The Labor-Optimized Hybrid Policy (F1 Weekdays + F2 Weekends):
    Adopt if floor space cannot accommodate a serpentine queue:
    • Weekdays: Deploy Framework 1 (Dynamic Counter). Mixed basket traffic flows naturally across dynamic counters, keeping labor costs low (saving ~40% cashier hours).
    • Weekends: Switch to Framework 2 (Dedicated Express Lane). Protects small-basket transactions from cart-loading shoppers with negative binomial baskets, cutting weekend peak wait times by 44.7% vs. Dynamic Counter.
